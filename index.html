The world is asking

Who should make the rules for AI?

Artificial intelligence is already part of ordinary life. It can help us learn, translate, find information, study science, and help doctors. Used carelessly, it can also cause harm.

In September 2026, leaders at the United Nations discussed how countries should make AI safer and fairer. The UN says AI should be safe, transparent, and accountable, with human dignity at the centre.

Many developing countries say a few powerful countries and companies should not write all the rules. They want a voice, and they want access to computers, skills, and knowledge so they are not left behind.

Think of a powerful new machine at school. You would still want rules: use it for learning, check its answers, do not bully, do not share private information, do not cheat, and ask an adult when something is unclear. AI needs safeguards of the same kind.

AI can help in health, education, farming, weather, and science. It can also spread fake pictures and stories, invade privacy, make unfair decisions, and be misused. That is why the rules matter.

How India plans to move clean electricity

A green energy highway

A solar panel makes electricity when the sun is out. The electricity still has to travel to a school, a home, or a factory. That is the job of power lines.

A Green Energy Corridor is a network that carries electricity from solar power, wind power, and hydropower. Think of it as a highway for electricity.

The Union Cabinet has approved Green Energy Corridor Phase-III. It is planned to help India move up to 135 GW of renewable energy across states and Union Territories, and to be completed by 2032–33. One gigawatt is 1,000 megawatts. For a young reader, 135 GW simply means a huge amount of clean energy.

Solar panels rest at night. The project includes 50 GWh of battery storage, so extra electricity can be kept and used later, the way a water bottle holds a drink for later.

The project is costed at about ₹1.86 lakh crore. About ₹50,000 crore is planned for batteries. Clean power still has to reach homes, schools, hospitals, factories, and trains.

India and New Zealand

A new trade partnership

India and New Zealand have signed a Free Trade Agreement set to come into force on 20 October 2026.

Imagine one country sending toys to another. A customs duty is an extra tax at the border. An FTA can reduce or remove some of those taxes.

Under this agreement, 100% of Indian exports are to receive zero-duty access to New Zealand. Sectors expected to benefit include textiles, leather, engineering, pharmaceuticals, agricultural products, and processed food. New Zealand has committed to facilitating US$20 billion of investment in India over 15 years. The two countries also aim to lift bilateral trade to around ₹35,000 crore over four to five years.

India has kept sensitive areas, including dairy and several farm products, protected. A trade deal is not “remove every tax on everything.”

Think about it.

25 September 2026

12 years of Make in India

Make in India was launched in 2014 to encourage manufacturing, invention, investment, and new businesses. On 25 September 2026 it completed 12 years.

Making a school bag takes a design, materials, workers, machines, packing, and a shop. That process is manufacturing. The programme now covers 27 major sectors, from cars and electronics to medicines, railways, steel, and defence.

Electronics production grew from about ₹2 lakh crore in 2014–15 to around ₹13 lakh crore in 2025–26. Mobile-phone production grew from about ₹19,000 crore to around ₹6 lakh crore.

In the first four months of 2026–27, manufacturing activity grew by about 7%. If a factory made 100 units last year, it made about 107 in the same period this year.

The path the paper gives young makers is idea, design, test, make, improve.

Junior News ExpressWorld Tourism Day

27 September · AI joins the journey

India is full of places.

World Tourism Day celebrates travel and the people and cultures you meet. In 2026 the theme is digital technology and artificial intelligence in tourism. An AI helper might suggest a two-day trip for someone who loves animals, nature, and history. People still make the final choice, and private information stays private.

India holds the Himalayas, the beaches of Goa and Kerala, tiger reserves, the forts of Rajasthan, temples, festivals, and a different kitchen in every state.

In 2025, people made more than 4 billion tourist visits within India, and the country welcomed about 25 million foreign tourist visits. Chefs, drivers, guides, and artists earn a living from those journeys.

A smart traveller avoids plastic waste, uses a dustbin, leaves plants and animals alone, and does not damage a monument. Take memories. Leave the place clean.

Junior News ExpressMoney smart

Need or want?

Do I really need this?

A need is something important for health, safety, learning, or everyday life: food, clean water, a safe home, basic clothes, medicine, school books. If your school shoes are broken, replacing them can be a need. Carrying a water bottle from home can meet a need without a new drink every day.

A want is something you would like, and can usually live without: a toy, a video game, fancy clothes, an extra snack. A strong school bag that still works makes a new character bag a want. Advertisements and friends can make a want feel urgent. Wait a day. Sometimes the feeling fades.

A safer India for everyone

Why safety is shared

Imagine a city where every girl and woman can walk to school, travel, work, and play without fear. Women are almost half of India. Worry about safety can still decide where they go and which chances they take.

India has Women Help Desks, One Stop Centres, Fast Track Special Courts, Safe City projects, the Nirbhaya Fund, and the emergency number 112. A law on paper does not light a broken streetlight. Safety also needs light, reliable transport, quick help, and responsible behaviour.

Crime in India 2024, cited from the NCRB, recorded 4,41,534 cases of crimes against women. Women make up only about 10% of the police force. More women in public-facing jobs can make help easier to ask for.

Children can respect personal space, refuse to bully, speak up, ask a trusted adult, and treat girls and boys equally.

Gender equality

Equal chances

Gender equality means girls and boys get the same chance to learn, play, lead, and be respected. The UN Gender Snapshot 2026 says progress is real and still too slow. None of the gender-equality targets under Sustainable Development Goal 5 are on track for 2030.

In 2026, women held about 27% of seats in national parliaments, up from about 22% in 2015. That is still far from half.

Women and girls spend about 2.5 times as many hours as men and boys on unpaid cooking, cleaning, and care. Housework can belong to everyone in the house.

The same report says nearly 1 in 5 girls worldwide is married before 18. Protecting a child’s chance to learn and choose matters in every country.

A class leader for a day should not be reserved for boys or for girls. Interest can choose a future. Gender should not.

2 October 1869 · Porbandar

Gandhi’s journey

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi became known as Mahatma Gandhi. His public life was a series of experiments with truth and non-violence.

In 1893 he went to South Africa as a lawyer. Thrown out of a first-class railway seat despite a valid ticket, he began to practise satyagraha: standing against injustice with truth, not violence. He returned to India in 1915.

Champaran, 1917. He investigated indigo farmers in Bihar and supported them peacefully. Kheda, 1918. After crop failure, he supported farmers in Gujarat who asked for relief from land revenue.

Non-cooperation, 1920–22. He asked Indians to withdraw cooperation from British institutions and foreign cloth, and to wear khadi. He stopped the movement in 1922 after violence at Chauri Chaura.

Salt March, 1930. From 12 March he walked about 390 kilometres from Sabarmati to Dandi, and on 6 April broke the salt law. Quit India, 1942. His call was “Do or Die.”

Ideas that travelled in print

Gandhi the publisher

Gandhi knew a newspaper could carry an idea further than one speech. The Gandhi Heritage Portal records journals he owned, edited, or published.

Indian Opinion began in South Africa in 1903 and discussed the problems of the Indian community there.

Young India was his English weekly. His autobiography, The Story of My Experiments with Truth, began appearing in it in 1925.

Navajivan was published in Gujarati and later in Hindi, on society, education, and self-reliance.

Harijan began as a weekly on 11 February 1933. It argued against untouchability. Related papers included Harijan Bandhu and Harijan Sevak.
